# DriverCompass — Mission and Positioning

**Date:** 13 November 2026
**Status:** current statement of intent; the product is aligned to it in stages, not claimed to have reached it.

## 1. Mission

> Give professional drivers the information, independence and voice to choose who they work for.

**Brand:** DriverCompass — *Know the employer. Choose your next move.*
**Positioning:** the independent career and employer intelligence platform for professional drivers.
**Philosophy:** built around drivers.

## 2. The three pillars

### A. Know the employer
Employment intelligence, not vacancy marketing: structured conditions, verification that states what was checked, driver experiences, company replies, and no way to buy a better reputation.

### B. Show your value
A professional profile that carries licences, Code 95, ADR specialisations, transport categories, routes, equipment, languages and availability — with every claim labelled by how it was established. No arbitrary driver score.

### C. Choose directly
Discovery, information, trust and connection are ours. The employment decision is the driver's. No allocation, no ranking sold to employers, no intermediary between the driver and the company.

## 3. The competitive contrast

> Other platforms primarily help employers find drivers. DriverCompass also helps drivers find employers worth working for.

The contrast is a **combination**, not a feature list:

1. Independent employer intelligence (conditions + verified experiences + replies).
2. A driver-owned professional profile.
3. A transparent direct marketplace.

Any one of these is commoditised. Fyndaro, TruckersHUB, Coramelo and TIRwork each advertise overlapping pieces — profiles, verification, direct contact, matching. The differentiation has to be the combination and the integrity of the evidence, not the existence of a feature.

**The strongest single idea: promise versus reality.** An advert states what a company promises; DriverCompass records what drivers report and makes the gap visible.

## 4. What we refuse to do

- Sell, rank or hide reviews.
- Sell driver data, CVs or contact details.
- Reduce a driver to a popularity or suitability score.
- Present a self-declared qualification as verified.
- Present an unverified opinion as fact.
- Add navigation entries for features that do not exist.
- Claim legal compliance of any kind without a qualified review.

## 5. How the public site expresses the mission

| Pillar | Surface | Current expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Know the employer | Homepage hero + employer intelligence section; company profiles; vacancy records | Hero states the promise; the intelligence section explains verified / company-reply / not-verified and links to employers |
| Show your value | "Your experience has value" section; driver profile | Section lists the capability set and links to registration; profile form already captures the fields |
| Choose directly | "Connect directly. Keep control." section; enquiry flow | Section states control rules; enquiry flow exists and is driver-initiated |

**Navigation** is `Find work · Employers · How verification works · For employers`. Driver Reviews and Driver Profiles are deliberately *not* in the navigation: there is no reviews index page and no public driver directory yet, and the brief forbids empty navigation entries. They enter the navigation when the pages exist.
